What do the two days look like?

Day 1: plan, build, then show and tell.

  1. Day 1 · Wednesday morningWelcome

    15 minutes on the day, the starter kit and Codex.

  2. Day 1 · Wednesday morningPlan

    75 to 90 minutes to write a one-page plan with Codex. Your team lead signs it off.

  3. Day 1 · after lunchBuild

    First, a live link with sample data. Then connect it to real data.

  4. Day 1 · last hourShow and tell

    Four minutes per team, live link only. What we showed, a dead end, and what we decide for tomorrow.

And what happens on Day 2?

Day 2: build, swap, then play it back.

  1. Day 2 · Thursday, 09:00Build and test

    Keep going. Aim to be connected to data.

  2. Day 2 · mid-morningFresh-eyes swap

    About 45 minutes. Try another team's prototype, then sort the feedback.

  3. Day 2 · after lunch, to 15:00Build, fix, polish

    Feature freeze at 14:00: no new features. Then tidy up and fill in the team card.

  4. Day 2 · 15:00Playback and close

    75 minutes. A live demo from each team, then we pull it together.

What counts as done?

Three tiers, and a minimum.

  • Tier 1: SampleIt works with sample or made-up data.
  • Tier 2: ConnectedIt reads from real data: a public source that updates live, or a dataset you design and build yourselves.
  • Tier 3: ExtraSomething more, like an AI helper or saving new entries.

Every team aims for a live link and tier 2. Tier 3 is a bonus.

How should we start on Day 1?

Plan first, then build.

  • Give it 75 to 90 minutes. On the first morning, plan before you build anything.
  • Let Codex interview you. Ask it about who this is for, what problem it solves, and what data you will use.
  • End with a one-page plan. Keep it in your project. Your team lead signs it off in five minutes.

There are lots of planning tools out there. The starter kit lists some. Try one, or just use Codex.

How do we get the most out of Codex?

Two settings: model and effort.

  • Start light. The lightest model and lowest effort that does the job. Step up when you get stuck.
  • More effort costs more. It's slower and uses more of your allowance.
  • Be specific. Point Codex at the right files and ask for a plan before it edits.

How do we work together on GitHub?

Share one project, without overwriting each other.

GitHub is where your team's project lives online, so everyone can work on it at once.

  • Work on your own copyThis is called a branch. It is like a duplicate Figma page: you work on it without touching the original.
  • Review before it joinsAsk a teammate to look over your changes before they join the main version.
  • Ask when it looks wrongYour facilitators and team leads are there to help. Pause before you throw anything away.

Who does what in a team?

Four roles, shared and swapped.

  • Git stewardLooks after the shared project and the saving steps.
  • Data scoutFinds and shapes the data.
  • Prompt leadDrives Codex.
  • Tester and storytellerTries the prototype and prepares the playback.

Each person takes one main role. Everyone still gets hands-on, and you can swap during the two days.

Where does your data live?

Your choice. Keep it simple.

  • Start hereA simple fileA JSON or CSV file in your project. JSON is a tidy text list. CSV is a spreadsheet saved as text.
  • One computerBrowser storageYour browser's own storage, for data that only needs to stay on one computer.
  • For the curiousAn online databaseA service that keeps data on the internet. Not needed for this event.

Use your own data, made-up data or a mix. Anything you publish is public, so publish made-up data only. Keep real personal information on your own computer.

How do we keep things safe?

Keep it private. Check what AI gives you.

  • No passwords or keysNever put passwords or keys (secret codes for other services) in your project. They are public the moment you publish.
  • No confidential detailsDo not use confidential client or bid details. Follow CGI guidance on AI.
  • Check everythingDo not invent skills, achievements or evidence. Check what the AI gives you.

What is the fresh-eyes swap?

Day 2: try another team's work with fresh eyes.

  1. 5 minutes · whole roomCheck in

    Team leads mark their team on a shared board: is it live, is it connected to data, what will be done by 14:00?

  2. 25 minutesSwap

    Two of you try another team's prototype cold. Note what confused you, what worked, and one idea.

  3. 15 minutes · own teamSort

    Sort feedback into must fix, nice to have, and ignore. Only must fix goes into the afternoon.

What do we show at the end?

Show it live. No slides.

  • A live link and a live demo. Five minutes, then three for questions.
  • Fill in the team card in the polish hour, 14:00 to 15:00. It is on screen for the last minute.

Team card

  • Team:
  • The most important thing we learned:
  • Something we are wary of (and why):
  • The business benefit:For [who], this [saves / enables / improves] [what], and we saw [evidence]. To use it for real we would need [one thing].
  • What AI did, and what we checked ourselves:

How do we pull it all together?

Pull it together on the wall.

  • LearnedThe most important thing each team learned.
  • WaryWhat each team is wary of, and why.
  • Business benefitWho it helps, and what changes for them.

Each team adds its cards to the wall. Everyone gets three dots to vote for what rings true. We read out the top themes, then everyone names one thing they will try at work in the next two weeks.
